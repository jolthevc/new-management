# Swing Question Examples

Status: Living reference  
Purpose: Show the difference between generic diligence and a question that tests the Bet.

A Swing Question is not merely an important diligence item.

It is one of the three to seven unknowns whose answer could materially change the investment view.

Each question should include:

- the question;
- why it matters;
- evidence needed;
- encouraging answer;
- concerning answer;
- decision impact.

## 1. Recurring landscaping maintenance

### Generic

> What is customer concentration?

### Swing Question

**How sticky is the maintenance book, and is retention contractual or habitual?**

**Why it matters:** The Bet assumes the recurring base is durable enough to support owner-independent cash flow and route-density economics.

**Evidence needed:** Revenue by customer for two to three years, contract versus non-contract maintenance, renewal and termination terms, retention by cohort, property-manager exposure.

**Encouraging:** Broad customer base, low churn, staggered renewals, limited annual rebid exposure.

**Concerning:** Cancellable monthly work concentrated in a few property managers who rebid annually.

**Decision impact:** Could materially reprice the deal.

## 2. Equipment-heavy service business

### Generic

> What equipment does the company own?

### Swing Question

**Is the stated equipment value real downside protection, or an accounting number attached to old assets that will soon require replacement?**

**Why it matters:** The Bear Case relies on the fleet protecting part of the purchase price.

**Evidence needed:** Equipment schedule, age, hours, ownership and liens, maintenance history, expected replacement timing, orderly liquidation value.

**Encouraging:** Late-model owned equipment with liquid resale markets and no major replacement cycle approaching.

**Concerning:** Old fleet, material liens, deferred maintenance, or replacement capex due soon.

**Decision impact:** Could kill the downside-protection thesis.

## 3. Owner-operated service business

### Generic

> How many hours does the owner work?

### Swing Question

**Where does the business depend on the owner's judgment, relationships, or tacit knowledge, and can those functions actually transfer?**

**Why it matters:** The Year 0 reconstruction assumes a hired manager can replace the owner's work without disturbing revenue.

**Evidence needed:** Weekly responsibilities, approval limits, manager tenure and authority, customer and vendor relationships held personally, exception-handling process.

**Encouraging:** Managers already run daily operations; owner mostly reviews financials and handles occasional exceptions.

**Concerning:** Owner sells, prices, resolves major jobs, recruits key labor, and owns the most important customer relationships.

**Decision impact:** Could materially reprice or kill a remote-ownership thesis.

## 4. Route-based home service

### Generic

> What is the service area?

### Swing Question

**Is the recurring customer book geographically dense enough for growth to create operating leverage, or is the route network too spread out?**

**Why it matters:** The positive thesis depends on incremental customers increasing stops per technician faster than they increase drive time.

**Evidence needed:** Customer map, route territories, stops per technician per day, drive time between jobs, revenue by ZIP code.

**Encouraging:** Large clusters of customers in adjacent neighborhoods with route capacity still available.

**Concerning:** Wide territory, low stop density, or growth requiring proportional additions of trucks and technicians.

**Decision impact:** Could materially strengthen or weaken the New Management Case.

## 5. Small manufacturer

### Generic

> What is capacity utilization?

### Swing Question

**Is the existing equipment base scarce productive capacity with room to absorb more volume, or is the apparent spare capacity actually constrained by labor, setup time, scrap, or a bottleneck machine?**

**Why it matters:** The growth case assumes new revenue can use assets already paid for.

**Evidence needed:** Machine hours, utilization by work center, setup time, scrap, overtime, lead times, bottleneck process, operator availability.

**Encouraging:** Low utilization caused by insufficient sales with labor and bottleneck capacity available.

**Concerning:** "Spare capacity" exists only on non-bottleneck machines while labor or one critical work center is already full.

**Decision impact:** Could materially strengthen or kill the operating-leverage thesis.

## 6. B2B recurring service

### Generic

> What is churn?

### Swing Question

**What actually holds customers in place: contract term, workflow integration, switching cost, service quality, or simple inertia?**

**Why it matters:** The valuation case gives the recurring revenue a premium only if the mechanism supporting retention is durable through ownership change.

**Evidence needed:** Contract terms, renewal structure, customer tenure, retention by cohort, implementation burden, termination behavior.

**Encouraging:** Multiyear agreements, high switching friction, long tenure, and retention that does not depend on the owner.

**Concerning:** Month-to-month relationships held together by owner relationships or annual price concessions.

**Decision impact:** Could materially reprice the business.

## Final test

A good Swing Question should make clear why one answer changes the deal and another does not.

If the question would appear unchanged in a generic diligence checklist for every company, it is probably not yet a Swing Question.
