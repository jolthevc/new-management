# Memo and Article Anatomy

Status: Canonical v1 governance  
Primary users: memo writer, media writer, publishing workflow.

## One act of thinking, two views

The memo and article come from the same intellectual work.

The memo retains analytical scaffolding.

The article removes much of that scaffolding, changes architecture and emphasis, adds a hook, and creates a smoother public reading experience.

The memo must already be written at publication quality.

Its strongest passages should generally be reusable in the article with little or no rewriting.

The media writer invents presentation, never substance.

## Memo anatomy

The first page carries the judgment. The rest earns it.

The default structure is:

### 1. The Listing

Source card:

- business as described;
- location as stated;
- asking price;
- revenue;
- stated SDE or EBITDA;
- marketplace;
- exact listing link.

Also include the transaction setup and any useful archetype or overlay.

### 2. Quality Card

Applicable attributes only.

Include:

- score;
- crisp reason;
- evidence caveat where material;
- "This business is really about" line.

### 3. Our View

Open with The Bet.

Explain:

- why this one is interesting;
- what we think is really going on;
- the one or two things that matter most;
- biggest current concern;
- what is unusual here.

### 4. The Business

Explain:

- what it does;
- who pays;
- how money is made;
- how the company wins work;
- what the owner appears to do.

Teach through the particulars.

### 5. Business Physics

Show:

- driving variables;
- what binds;
- what is scarce.

### 6. The Reconstructed P&L

Three columns:

- As Listed;
- Year 0, Our View;
- New Management Case, Year 3.

Show:

- row states;
- evidence coverage;
- reconstruction confidence;
- Earnings Keep Rate;
- Owner Replacement Tax where applicable;
- relevant margins and cash conversion.

### 7. Market and Competitive Position

Only material context.

Cover what changes the investment read:

- category structure;
- local competitive environment where relevant;
- demand drivers;
- pricing power;
- whether external tailwinds actually reach this business.

### 8. Under New Management

Day 1, Year 1, Year 3.

Include the earnings bridge.

Each material lever should be specific enough to understand:

- mechanism;
- impact;
- cost;
- timeline;
- confidence;
- first action;
- what has to be true;
- why it may remain uncaptured.

### 9. The Bear Case

At full strength.

Explain:

- how the Bet could be wrong;
- concentrated fragilities;
- what is left if wrong.

### 10. What Has to Be True

Open with:

> The deal really turns on: ...

Then provide three to seven thesis-specific Swing Questions with decision impact.

### 11. Ownership

Explain:

- feasible ownership structures;
- what must transfer;
- what the next owner must be unusually good at;
- whether a management layer exists or must be built.

### 12. The Price

Include:

- headline multiple;
- market grounding;
- implied effective multiple;
- indicative Year 0 value range;
- preliminary maximum price;
- financing summary;
- right-buyer framing.

Close with:

**Did the Bet survive?**

## Memo appendices

As useful:

- Evidence Ledger;
- baseline diligence checklist;
- Sources and Evidence Snapshot;
- deal model;
- category assumptions used.

Appendices support the argument. They do not substitute for explaining it.

## Memo length

No hard page target.

Roughly 2,500 to 4,000 words may be common, excluding appendices and tables.

Use the space the analysis earns.

## Article anatomy

The article is a narrative transformation of the approved memo.

Default backbone:

1. opening hook;
2. The Listing;
3. what kind of business this is;
4. how the machine works;
5. what it actually earns;
6. The Bet;
7. Under New Management;
8. What Has to Be True;
9. how we are wrong;
10. what we would pay;
11. reader poll.

This is not a fixed visible template.

Sections should merge, move, expand, or disappear when the story demands it.

Do not make every issue feel architecturally identical.

## The source card

Near the top of every article:

**The listing.** [Business type], [location as stated]. Asking [price] · Revenue [revenue] · SDE/EBITDA [earnings]. Listed on [marketplace] · View original listing →

If the listing has disappeared:

**listing since removed**

The article stands on the preserved snapshot.

## Article sourcing rules

The public article:

- names or links the real public listing by default;
- restates public facts in New Management's own language;
- marks our estimates as ours;
- does not reproduce listing prose, broker copy, screenshots, or photographs;
- never uses confidential information.

The approved memo is canonical.

The media writer receives only approved memo substance plus approved exhibits, source card, and memo source map.

## Analytical exhibits

Three core exhibits are rendered deterministically from structured analysis:

### Quality Card

Readable, compact, explanatory.

### Reconstructed P&L

The financial backbone.

The public exhibit retains the **evidence coverage** and **reconstruction confidence** line. Removing the internal scaffolding does not mean hiding how much of the reconstruction is observed versus assumed.

### Earnings bridge

The value-creation bridge from Year 0 to the New Management Case.

The exhibits should feel visually consistent with the publication, but clarity outranks personality.

## AI-image opportunities

Public articles may include roughly two to four generated editorial-image opportunities when the story earns them.

Roles:

- SCENE;
- MECHANISM;
- TRANSFORMATION.

The media writer inserts the brief at the natural point in the prose.

Standard form:

**[AI IMAGE — ROLE]**

**Idea:** The visual concept and what the reader should understand or feel.  
**Purpose:** Why this earns a visual break.  
**Style:** New Management house illustration style from governance/03_editorial_standard.md.  
**Avoid:** Company-specific or documentary implications and any scene-specific exclusions.  
**Aspect:** Usually 3:2 landscape.

The memo does not require editorial AI images by default.

## Visual pacing

Images and exhibits are part of editorial rhythm.

Use them to:

- reset the eye;
- explain a mechanism;
- make numbers easier to absorb;
- make the physical business feel tangible.

Do not place them mechanically every N words.

A long prose section with no natural visual opportunity is allowed.

A short article with three excellent exhibits may need little generated art.

## Article length

Most public pieces will naturally land around 1,800 to 2,600 words of body prose.

This is directional only.

A simple business may earn 1,400 to 1,700.

A rich business may earn 3,000 or more.

Never cut insight to hit a count.

Never pad thin analysis to reach one.

## Closing poll

Default:

**Would you buy it?**

The poll is the reader's decision, not ours.

## Final anatomy test

The reader should feel a story unfolding, not a template being completed.

The furniture should make the thinking easier to absorb.

It should never become the product.
