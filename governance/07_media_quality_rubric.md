# Media Quality Rubric

Status: Canonical v1 governance  
Primary user: media scorer.

## Purpose

The claim checker asks:

**Can this article hold water externally?**

The media scorer asks:

**Is this article actually excellent?**

Factual cleanliness is a prerequisite, not a quality score.

The scorer evaluates the clean article against the approved memo.

It does not reward unsupported creativity.

It does not rewrite.

## Publish-quality threshold

Publish quality requires both:

- **85 / 100 total or higher**
- **Investment Rigor: at least 16 / 20**
- **Thesis and Insight: at least 16 / 20**
- **Business Understanding and Explanatory Value: at least 16 / 20**

Narrative, prose, and distinctiveness may trade off more freely.

The thresholds are calibratable through the first five fixtures.

## 1. Investment Rigor — 20 points

Question:

**Does the article preserve the quality, nuance, uncertainty, and economic logic of the approved underwriting?**

### 20

The investment case is as strong in public form as in the memo. Important tradeoffs remain intact. Valuation, normalization, downside, and value creation are connected. Uncertainty is precise without weakening the view.

### 16

Strong enough to publish. The core underwriting survives with minor compression or simplification, but no material analytical distortion.

### 12

Competent but meaningfully thinner than the memo. Important nuance, downside, or valuation logic has been flattened.

### 8 or below

The article has become content about a deal rather than serious investment analysis.

## 2. Thesis and Insight — 20 points

Question:

**Is there a specific, interesting point of view? Does the piece surface non-obvious ideas rather than summarize?**

### 20

The article contains a clear thesis and multiple earned observations that change how a smart reader sees the business. Insights emerge from evidence and mechanism, not clever phrasing.

### 16

Specific and interesting. At least one memorable insight or mechanism is developed properly.

### 12

The conclusions are sensible but mostly expected. The article summarizes more than it discovers.

### 8 or below

Generic category observations could be transplanted to many businesses.

## 3. Business Understanding and Explanatory Value — 20 points

Question:

**Does the reader genuinely understand how this business works and learn something transferable?**

### 20

The reader can explain the economic machine, binding constraint, source of scarcity, and important cash mechanics after reading. Transferable lessons emerge naturally.

### 16

The operating model and key mechanism are clear. A reader learns something useful beyond the listing.

### 12

The article describes the business but does not fully expose why the economics behave as they do.

### 8 or below

The business remains an abstraction made of revenue, margin, and multiple.

## 4. Narrative Structure and Flow — 15 points

Question:

**Does the piece move naturally? Is every section earned rather than templated? Is it easy to navigate?**

### 15

The reader rarely notices structure because the next idea feels inevitable. Paragraph and section lengths vary naturally. Exhibits and images appear where they genuinely improve comprehension. Dense sections are given air. The piece is comfortable to read on a phone.

### 12

Strong flow with a few visible seams, overlong passages, or formulaic transitions.

### 9

Correct but audibly assembled. Sections feel like memo headings converted into prose. Paragraphing or visual density occasionally becomes tiring.

### 6 or below

The reader must work to continue. Walls of text, constant one-line paragraphs, repetitive section shapes, or unnecessary repetition damage the experience.

## 5. Prose and Enjoyability — 15 points

Question:

**Is the writing clear, lively, confident, smooth, and pleasurable to read?**

### 15

The reader forgets they are reading investment analysis. The prose feels authored, specific, fluid, and effortless while carrying complex ideas. Sentence and paragraph rhythm varies naturally. The writing has personality without performing personality.

### 12

Strong and readable, with a few formulaic phrases or visible seams.

### 9

Competent but audibly assembled. The writing communicates information but rarely creates pleasure.

### 6 or below

Generic, mechanical, repetitive, jargon-heavy, over-signposted, or exhausting.

### Named failure modes

Check for:

- throat clearing;
- mechanical triplets;
- "it is worth noting";
- "from an investment perspective";
- lazy rhetorical questions;
- paragraph-ending restatements;
- hedge stacks;
- repeated "not X, but Y";
- symmetrical AI rhythm;
- generic summary endings;
- walls of text;
- constant one-line paragraphs.

These are diagnostic signals, not automatic deductions.

## 6. Distinctiveness and Memorability — 10 points

Question:

**Does this feel like New Management rather than generic investment content? Will the reader remember a formulation, mechanism, or visual idea?**

### 10

The piece has a recognizable point of view and at least one element a reader is likely to repeat or remember. Distinctiveness comes from what the analysis noticed, not from gimmicks.

### 8

Clearly above generic finance writing, with a recognizable voice and one or two distinctive observations.

### 6

Solid but interchangeable with good general business commentary.

### 4 or below

Could have been produced by any generic investment-content system.

## Scoring discipline

Do not grade on effort.

Do not reward length.

Do not reward the presence of named New Management frameworks by itself.

Do not reward cleverness that is not analytically useful.

Do not let beautiful prose compensate for weak underwriting.

Do not let rigorous underwriting compensate for an article nobody wants to read.

Scores should spread.

A merely good article should not automatically receive 90.

## Scorer output

The report contains:

- total score;
- six component scores;
- whether the total threshold passes;
- whether each analytical floor passes;
- strongest part of the article;
- weakest part;
- three highest-leverage editorial changes.

The three changes should be specific enough for the writer to execute.

Bad:

> Improve flow.

Better:

> The route-density explanation is the strongest idea in the piece, but it appears after two generic market paragraphs. Move it into the opening and cut the market setup to the one local-demand fact that changes the thesis.

## One automatic rewrite

If the clean article misses either:

- the 85 total;
- any analytical floor;

the writer receives the scorer's three highest-leverage changes and may perform one automatic editorial revision.

Then:

- claim check reruns;
- media score reruns.

There is no second automatic rewrite.

If the article still misses, send it to James with the score and critique.

## Visual quality within the score

There is no separate image score in v1.

Visual judgment is reflected in:

- Narrative Structure and Flow;
- Prose and Enjoyability.

An article with excellent sentences but exhausting visual density cannot receive full marks.

An article with decorative images that interrupt rather than improve the argument should also lose points.

## Final question

When scoring, ask:

**Did we turn a serious investment breakdown into something a curious, intelligent person would genuinely want to finish?**
