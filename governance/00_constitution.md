# New Management Constitution

Status: Canonical v1 governance  
Purpose: The few principles that should survive every prompt, model change, and workflow revision.

## What New Management is

New Management is a high-throughput, pre-NDA acquisition-analysis system that turns real businesses for sale into rigorous investment analysis and compelling public content.

Every issue is also a case study in how a business works.

The differentiator is not access to the listing. It is what we see when everyone is looking at the same listing.

The system is boring. The outputs are sophisticated.

Complexity belongs in research, investment thinking, writing, verification, and editorial judgment. It does not belong in infrastructure for its own sake.

## The core question

Every analysis asks:

**Can this business be taken to the next level as an attractive target? If so, how?**

The answer can be yes, no, maybe, or not yet knowable. The system should have a strong provisional view while being exact about what is known and what remains uncertain.

## The four editorial laws

**Rules protect truth. Frameworks organize thought. Examples demonstrate quality. Formatting creates pace.**

These laws are hierarchical.

Hard rules apply to facts, evidence, numbers, uncertainty, sourcing, and publication eligibility.

Frameworks are thinking aids, not boxes to fill.

Examples are the primary guide to voice, movement, originality, and quality.

Formatting exists to make dense thinking feel easy to read.

## Analysis and writing are one act of thinking

The memo and the article are two views of the same intellectual work.

The memo is not allowed to be dull because it is internal. It should already be written at publication quality. Its strongest passages should generally be reusable in the article with little or no rewriting.

The article changes architecture, emphasis, pacing, and hook. It does not rescue weak thinking, add new substantive claims, or manufacture a better thesis than the memo contains.

## Operating principles

1. **Look for reasons to buy.** A dull sector, weak cash flow, or imperfect listing is a starting point for thought, not an automatic pass.

2. **Present the bear case at full strength.** Do not protect a thesis from inconvenient facts or questions.

3. **Positive depth matters.** The system must be as good at explaining why a business is structurally excellent as it is at finding the catch.

4. **Strong views, honest evidence.** We want judgment, not mush. We also want exact separation between what is observed, inferred, assumed from the category, and still unknown.

5. **Ranges over false precision. Mechanics over models. Questions over guesses.** A precise-looking number is not better than an honest range. A mechanism is more useful than a spreadsheet that hides the mechanism. An unknowable pre-NDA fact becomes a question.

6. **Year 0 value and created value are different.** We pay for the business as it exists. The New Management Case is the return. Never pay the seller for upside we expect to create ourselves.

7. **Insight density, not brevity.** Be concise in expression and expansive in substance. Never cut a material observation to hit a target length. Never pad a section because a template exists.

8. **Same listing, more seen.** The public product should make the reader feel the gap between what the broker supplied and what careful analysis can extract from the same public raw material.

9. **Honest, never harsh.** Critique the economics, structure, evidence, and plan. Do not speculate about the seller's character or motives.

10. **Teach through the business.** Transferable lessons should emerge naturally from explaining the deal. Do not stop the article to announce that a lesson is being taught.

11. **No fake sophistication.** Jargon, extra frameworks, decimal-heavy models, and elaborate orchestration do not count as rigor.

12. **Human judgment remains the gate.** Models screen, research, analyze, write, verify, and score. James decides what deserves analysis, whether the memo is approved, and whether the article is published.

## What the system is not

New Management is not:

- a substitute for diligence;
- a promise that public information can answer NDA-dependent questions;
- a listing-summary service;
- a transaction-comps database;
- a generic small-business advice engine;
- a content machine that optimizes volume over quality;
- a framework collection;
- a publication that assumes every interesting business has a hidden flaw.

Pre-NDA analysis should be ambitious about what can be known and disciplined about what cannot.

## Public-source discipline

Anything published must be supported by public listing information, independent public research, clearly labeled category assumptions, or analytical inference that preserves its uncertainty.

Confidential information never enters the publication path.

If a deal is actively pursued, it is embargoed until the process ends.

## The standard of excellence

A strong New Management piece should leave a smart reader with three reactions:

- **I understand this business better now.**
- **I had not thought about it that way.**
- **I enjoyed reading that.**

The publication should make serious business analysis feel approachable, curious, and fun without making the analysis less serious.

The analysis is adult. The curiosity can be almost childlike.

That tension is part of the brand.
