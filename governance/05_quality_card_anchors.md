# Quality Card Anchors

Status: Canonical v1 governance  
Purpose: Make Quality Card scoring consistent across deals.

Use only attributes that materially apply.

Scores of 2 and 4 interpolate between the 1, 3, and 5 anchors.

The explanation is more important than the number.

## Gross Margin

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Thin or commodity economics, below a credible category norm. |
| 3 | Around category norm with no clear structural advantage. |
| 5 | Well above norm with an identifiable structural reason such as mix, density, scarcity, or pricing. |

## Normalized Profitability

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Year 0 cash earnings are near zero or negative after owner replacement and economic costs. |
| 3 | Normalized margin is around category norm. |
| 5 | Well above norm, cash-backed, and supported by an identifiable operating advantage. |

## Revenue Durability

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Primarily one-off or project work with low visibility. |
| 3 | Meaningful repeat behavior or backlog, but limited contractual protection. |
| 5 | Highly recurring or contracted, with strong retention and visibility supported by evidence. |

### Note on recurrence

Recurring revenue comes in grades.

The score should consider what holds the revenue in place:

- contract term;
- switching cost;
- embedded workflow;
- regulation;
- habit;
- technician or owner relationship;
- annual rebid exposure.

Do not give a high score merely because the listing uses the word recurring.

## Operating Leverage

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Costs largely scale one-for-one with revenue. |
| 3 | Some fixed base and modest incremental margin. |
| 5 | Large fixed base, density effect, or other mechanism causes incremental revenue to fall through at high margin. |

## Working-Capital Intensity

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Pays materially before collecting; long receivables, inventory, or project funding cause growth to consume cash. |
| 3 | Roughly balanced terms and ordinary working-capital needs. |
| 5 | Deposits, upfront payment, rapid collection, or negative working capital cause growth to generate cash. |

## Capital Intensity

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Heavy fleet, equipment, or facilities with continual replacement; maintenance capex consumes a large share of earnings. |
| 3 | Moderate maintenance capex around category norm. |
| 5 | Asset-light with negligible maintenance capex relative to earnings. |

## Demand Backdrop

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Secular decline or highly cyclical/discretionary demand with material local exposure. |
| 3 | Stable, replacement-driven, or roughly GDP-like demand. |
| 5 | Structural growth that can be shown to reach this company's actual local or end-market exposure. |

### Rule

A growing national market does not automatically create a 5.

The tailwind must reach this business.

## Natural Expansion

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Boxed in by geography, license, customer set, or product with no obvious adjacent move. |
| 3 | Plausible adjacencies that require meaningful new capability. |
| 5 | Obvious adjacent services, territory, or product sold through the same customers, crews, routes, assets, or channel. |

## Pricing Power

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Price-taker, bid-driven, highly shopped, or unable to pass through costs. |
| 3 | Can generally pass through cost inflation but has limited ability to expand margin. |
| 5 | Can raise price above inflation without meaningful volume loss for an identifiable reason such as scarcity, switching cost, regulation, reputation, or low customer attention. |

## Transferability

| Score | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 1 | Owner sells, prices, resolves exceptions, and holds key relationships; little second layer. |
| 3 | A management layer exists but the owner remains central to sales, relationships, pricing, or unusual decisions. |
| 5 | Team runs daily operations; owner mainly oversees; key knowledge and relationships are institutional rather than personal. |

## Evidence rules

A score should not imply more certainty than the evidence supports.

For each attribute:

- state the reason;
- identify the evidence class when material;
- flag any decisive unknown;
- point to a Swing Question when the score depends on an unverified inference.

Missing disclosure is not automatically a low score.

If the attribute cannot be responsibly scored, omit it or state that there is not enough evidence.

## "This business is really about"

After the card, write one line identifying the one or two attributes that define the economics.

Good:

> This business is really about revenue durability plus route density.

> This business is really about exceptional cash conversion offset by owner dependence.

Bad:

> This business is really about profitability and growth.

The summary should compress the economic machine, not repeat generic virtues.
